  • OpenStreetMap (OSM)

    Collaborative mapping, data quality, and stakeholder interactions.

Abstract: This study explores the complexities of OpenStreetMap (OSM) as a collaborative mapping platform, examining how data is imported, maintained, and utilized in various contexts. Our research began with an overview of OSM's data import processes and the challenges of community-driven contributions, highlighting how machine learning is increasingly used to correct inaccuracies. We then analyzed user engagement patterns, focusing on contributions during crisis response efforts, such as the 2013 Haiti earthquake.

To further understand OSM's role in urban planning, we examined case studies where government officials and city planners leveraged the platform for decision-making. Using the OSM Wiki, we identified how community members create and update map elements, revealing key stakeholder influences. Additionally, we constructed a data journey using Miro and developed a social worlds diagram to visualize interactions between stakeholders, data sources, and external influences at macro, meso, and micro levels.

Our analysis uncovered key friction points in OSM, including barriers to user contribution, inconsistencies in mapping accuracy due to varying experience levels, and challenges in keeping data up-to-date with new satellite imagery. These issues impact the reliability of OSM data, affecting researchers, humanitarian organizations, and policymakers who depend on accurate geographic information.
